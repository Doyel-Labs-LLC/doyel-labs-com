# Doyel Labs Privacy Policy

This policy belongs to Doyel Labs LLC, a Wyoming limited liability company in Casper, Wyoming.
It covers what happens to your information when you visit `doyel-labs.com`, when you contact
us, and when we build or host software for you. BAI has its own privacy notice at
`/legal/bai/privacy/`; ConnectionLoop's notice ships inside the app.

## When you visit this website

**We measure traffic, and this section says exactly how.** The paragraphs below are
selected automatically from the configuration the site was built with, so what you read
here matches what actually runs.

<!-- analytics:plausible -->
The public pages on `doyel-labs.com` use **Plausible Analytics** (plausible.io). It is the
only visitor analytics provider selected for this website.

- Plausible is cookieless: it sets no cookies on your device and stores no cross-session
  identifier.
- Plausible records page views, referrers, screen sizes, browsers, and coarse country from
  your IP address, which is discarded after the request.
- It does not record mouse movement, keystrokes, form values, page content, or replay
  sessions, and it is never used for advertising.
<!-- /analytics -->

<!-- analytics:cloudflare -->
The public pages on `doyel-labs.com` use **Cloudflare Web Analytics**. It is the only
visitor analytics provider selected for this website.

- Cloudflare's browser beacon measures page views, referral information, broad browser,
  device and operating-system categories, approximate country, and page-performance
  timings. These describe website activity, not named visitors.
- The beacon does not use cookies, local storage, persistent browser identifiers, or
  fingerprinting to track people across sites or sessions.
- Cloudflare receives the source IP address when handling a beacon request and states that
  its RUM service discards it at the nearest data center rather than retaining it. Our
  dashboard does not request, display, or store raw IP addresses.
- Cloudflare does not log URL query strings. Our dashboard also removes query strings and
  fragments and shows only referral hosts, never full referral URLs.
- Cloudflare keeps about six months of Web Analytics data. We do not create a separate
  visitor-event database from it.

We use these aggregate reports to understand which pages are useful. We do not use them
for advertising, retargeting, or linking contact inquiries to browsing activity.
Cloudflare's own [RUM privacy information](https://developers.cloudflare.com/speed/observatory/rum-beacon/#privacy-information)
describes its processing.

### Approximate city and region counts

We also keep **first-party approximate location counts** for published public pages. When
a page loads, your browser sends one same-origin request containing only the page path,
without query string, fragment, referrer, or cookies. The server validates and then
discards that path. Cloudflare attaches an approximate country, region, and city to the
request from its own network data; we never ask for GPS, a postal address, or coordinates.
VPNs and mobile networks can mislocate activity, and missing locations are grouped as
unknown.

Our Cloudflare D1 database stores only hourly country/region/city counters, a daily
admission total, and the hour collection first received data. It never stores raw events,
IP addresses, paths, referrers, user agents, visitor IDs, cookies, fingerprints, or
per-person profiles. These are page views, not unique visitors.

Collection honors the Global Privacy Control and Do Not Track signals your browser can send,
stops after 5,000 accepted page views per UTC day, and does not run on preview or
development hostnames. Hourly aggregates are retained for about 31 days and cleaned up
hourly. Cloudflare D1's built-in backups may retain deleted aggregates for up to
30 additional days.

**No session replay, no ad pixels.** There is no Google Analytics, Meta Pixel, Microsoft
Clarity, Hotjar, or any session-replay tool on this site, and we do not record form
values, keystrokes, or page content.

**Hosting.** The site is served by Cloudflare Pages. Cloudflare sees the same connection
data any web host sees (IP address, request headers) and applies its own privacy terms.

**Owner dashboard.** Aggregate reports are viewed on a private page protected by
Cloudflare Access, available only to the site owner. Visitor analytics is not collected
on that page.

## When you send the contact form

The form asks for your name (optional), email, what you need help with, a message, and
optionally good times for a call. Sending it triggers the following, and nothing else:

- **Cloudflare Turnstile** checks that a person, not a bot, submitted the form. Turnstile may
  read browser signals to do so; see Cloudflare's privacy policy.
- **A per-address limit** keeps one source from flooding the inbox. It stores a short-lived
  counter keyed to your IP address that expires within five minutes.
- **Resend** (resend.com) delivers the message as an email to `support@doyel-labs.com`, which
  is a Google Workspace mailbox read by people at Doyel Labs, not by an automated system.

Nothing from the form is written to a database, sold, shared for marketing, or added to a
list. We keep your message in the mailbox for as long as it takes to reply and, if we work
together, for the life of that work plus our normal business-record retention. You can ask
us to delete it at any time.

If you email or call us directly, the same applies.

## When we build or host software for you

**Websites.** If you take the care plan, we host your site. Your visitors' data is governed by
your own privacy notice, which we will write with you. We do not add trackers to your site.
Form submissions on your site go to your inbox, not ours.

**Custom software.** Each build runs in accounts you own, on infrastructure you own, unless
we agree in writing otherwise. Where we operate a system on your behalf, the written scope
names exactly what we hold, for how long, and how you get it back. For payroll workspaces,
see the separate data map at `/legal/payroll-data/`.

Bank routing numbers, account numbers, and card numbers are never stored by software we
build unless the written scope says so and names the safeguards.
